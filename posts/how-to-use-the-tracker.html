A podcast tracker only works if it's actually used. The best system in the world sits unused if it's confusing or requires too much effort to maintain. The Podcast Producer & Episode Management Tracker is designed to be simple: add a row for each episode, fill in key details as they become known, and reference it daily to stay on top of deadlines.

If you're new to tracking or considering switching from email-and-notes chaos, here's exactly how to use the tracker day-to-day.

The Core Columns

Date: This is typically the episode number's corresponding air date. If Episode 5 publishes on August 25th, this cell says "2026-08-25." This column is your primary sort and lets you scan weeks of upcoming episodes at a glance.

Episode Number: Sequential numbering (1, 2, 3, etc.) makes it easy to reference episodes in conversation. "Did we send the prep materials for Episode 12?" It's faster than saying "the one with the guest from Brooklyn."

Episode Title: The title that will appear in your RSS feed and on your website. You might not know this until after recording or during editing, so it's fine if this cell starts empty. Filling it in before show notes are written is helpful for keeping everything aligned.

Guest Name and Guest Email: Self-explanatory, but critical. If a guest emails you asking "when should I log in?" you look up their name in the tracker, find the recording date and time, and respond instantly. You don't dig through email threads.

Guest Confirmation Status: "Pending," "Confirmed," or "Declined." This single field tells you whether the episode slot is actually filled or if you need to keep outreaching. A column full of "Confirmed" entries shows you've got your pipeline locked in. A row with "Pending" after a week means you need to send a follow-up email.

Recording Date and Recording Time: The date and time you've scheduled with the guest. This is your internal deadline for being ready to record. If recording dates start to collide or bunch up (three episodes recorded in one day), you can see it and spread them out before hitting the wall.

Editing Deadline: When this episode must be done editing. If your typical edit takes 3 days and you record on Monday, the editing deadline is Thursday. This date helps you manage workload. If you've got three editing deadlines on Friday, you know Wednesday is going to be tight.

Publish Date: When the episode goes live. This is the promise to your audience. Lock this in weeks in advance and build your production timeline around it.

Publish Status: "Not yet," "In progress," "Published." This tells you which episodes have actually gone live. If you're running multiple shows, you can filter this column to see which show's episodes are still pending publication.

Sponsorship Deal: The name or type of sponsorship attached to this episode (e.g., "Acme Tools" or "Affiliate: $X per link"). If you're not running sponsors, leave it blank. If you are, this column ensures you remember which episodes have paid promotions and which are organic.

Affiliate Links: URLs or references to any affiliate links mentioned in this episode. Tracking them here lets you go back later and verify they were included in show notes and actually went live.

Episode Length (minutes): How long the final published episode is. This is metadata your distribution platform needs, and tracking it helps you spot patterns. If most of your episodes run 40-50 minutes but one is 70, that's useful feedback about pacing or guest chemistry.

Show Notes Link: A link to where your show notes live (your website, Notion, Google Doc, etc.). This is a quick reference to jump to full episode details without digging through folders.

Promotion Status: "Not started," "In progress," or "Complete." Did you share clips? Send the episode to your email list? Post about it on social media? This column flags which episodes have actually been promoted versus which are sitting quietly in the feed.

Daily Workflow

Monday Morning: Review the tracker. Which episodes are being recorded this week? Make sure guests are confirmed. Check that editing deadlines are coming up and confirm the editor has the raw files. Look at publish dates this week and confirm show notes are being written.

Wednesday: A guest emails you about recording time. Look up their name, grab the recording date and time from the tracker, send confirmation. Takes 30 seconds instead of five minutes of email searching.

Thursday: Recording session happens. Mark down episode length and any notes (guest was great but we had technical glitches, for example). Make sure the editor has the file immediately after recording.

Friday: Check editing deadline. Is the episode done? If yes, start show notes. If no, confirm with editor what's blocking it. Update publish status to "In progress." Schedule promotion (clips, emails, social posts).

Sunday Night: Verify the episode published successfully. Update publish status to "Published." Make sure affiliate links are in show notes. Add listener count if you have early metrics. Close out this episode and look ahead to next week.

Making It Stick

The tracker only works if you check it regularly—ideally daily or every other day. Set a recurring calendar reminder for Monday morning: "Review podcast tracker." That five-minute review prevents most problems. You catch missed confirmations, see editing deadlines coming up, and spot scheduling conflicts before they become disasters.
