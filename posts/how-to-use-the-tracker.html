A podcast tracker only works if it's actually used. The best system in the world sits unused if it's confusing or requires too much effort to maintain.

The Podcast Producer & Episode Management Tracker is designed to be simple: add a row for each episode, fill in key details as they become known, and reference it daily to stay on top of deadlines. If you're new to tracking or switching from email-and-notes chaos, here's exactly how to use it day-to-day.

The Core Columns

The columns fall into four groups: identifying the episode, the guest, the schedule, and what happens around publishing.

Episode basics

  • Date. The episode's air date. If Episode 5 publishes on August 25th, this cell says "2026-08-25." It's your primary sort and lets you scan weeks of upcoming episodes at a glance.
  • Episode Number. Sequential numbering (1, 2, 3, etc.) makes episodes easy to reference. "Did we send the prep materials for Episode 12?" is faster than "the one with the guest from Brooklyn."
  • Episode Title. The title that will appear in your RSS feed and on your website. It's fine if this starts empty until recording or editing. Filling it in before show notes are written keeps everything aligned.

Guest details

  • Guest Name and Guest Email. Self-explanatory, but critical. If a guest asks "when should I log in?", you look up their name, find the recording date and time, and respond instantly. You don't dig through email threads.
  • Guest Confirmation Status. "Pending," "Confirmed," or "Declined." This tells you whether the slot is actually filled or you need to keep outreaching. A row still "Pending" after a week means you need to send a follow-up email.

Revenue, metadata and promotion

  • Sponsorship Deal. The name or type of sponsorship (e.g., "Acme Tools" or "Affiliate: $X per link"). Leave it blank if you're not running sponsors. If you are, it shows which episodes have paid promotions and which are organic.
  • Affiliate Links. URLs or references to affiliate links mentioned in the episode, so you can later verify they were included in show notes and actually went live.
  • Episode Length (minutes). Metadata your distribution platform needs. If most episodes run 40-50 minutes but one is 70, that's useful feedback about pacing or guest chemistry.
  • Show Notes Link. Where your show notes live (your website, Notion, Google Doc, etc.), so you can jump straight to full episode details.
  • Promotion Status. "Not started," "In progress," or "Complete." Did you share clips, email your list, post on social media? This flags which episodes were promoted and which are sitting quietly in the feed.

Daily Workflow

Here's how the tracker fits into a typical production week:

WhenWhat to do in the tracker
Monday morningReview the week. Which episodes are recording? Are guests confirmed? Are editing deadlines coming up, and does the editor have the raw files? Are show notes being written for this week's publish dates?
WednesdayA guest emails about recording time. Look up their name, grab the date and time, send confirmation. 30 seconds instead of five minutes of email searching.
ThursdayRecording session. Note episode length and any notes (great guest, technical glitches). Get the file to the editor immediately.
FridayCheck the editing deadline. Done? Start show notes. Not done? Confirm with the editor what's blocking it. Set publish status to "In progress" and schedule promotion (clips, emails, social posts).
Sunday nightVerify the episode published. Set status to "Published," check affiliate links are in show notes, add early listener counts if you have them, and look ahead to next week.

Making It Stick

The tracker only works if you check it regularly, ideally daily or every other day.

Set one recurring reminder

Add a recurring calendar reminder for Monday morning: "Review podcast tracker." That five-minute review prevents most problems. You catch missed confirmations, see editing deadlines coming up, and spot scheduling conflicts before they become disasters.
