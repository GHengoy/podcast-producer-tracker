Production Timeline: Managing Recording, Editing, and Publishing Dates

2026-08-17

← Back to the Complete Podcast Production Guide

Every podcast episode follows a timeline: it starts with a recording session, moves through editing, gets wrapped in show notes, and finally publishes to your feed. The trouble is, each step has its own deadline, and they're all interconnected. If recording runs late, editing gets squeezed. If editing takes longer than expected, publishing gets pushed back. If you're not tracking these deadlines explicitly, episodes miss their publish dates regularly.

The real challenge emerges when you're running multiple episodes in parallel. You might have one episode in editing while another is being recorded and a third is scheduled to publish tomorrow. Without explicit tracking of each phase for each episode, you lose track of which episode is where in the pipeline. Something falls through, a deadline gets missed, and your listeners notice the episode didn't arrive when they expected it.

The Production Pipeline

Think of your production workflow as a series of phases, each with a start date, an end date, and a deadline. Here's the pattern most podcasters follow:

Recording Phase: You and your guest meet and record the episode. This might be a single session or multiple sessions if recording long-form conversations. What matters for tracking: the recording date (or date range if multi-part), and how long the raw recording is. This tells you how much editing work is coming.

Editing Phase: The raw recording gets edited—silence removed, intros/outros added, levels balanced, any issues fixed. The editing deadline is typically 3-7 days after recording, depending on how complex your show is and how much outsourcing you use. You need to track the editing deadline explicitly so you know when this phase must be complete.

Show Notes Phase: While editing happens (or after), you write show notes—episode summary, timestamps for key topics, links to resources mentioned, guest information. Show notes often depend on the edited episode, so this phase overlaps with editing. The show notes deadline is usually 1-2 days before publish date.

Publishing Phase: Your episode goes live to your feed, gets uploaded to distribution platforms, and is announced to your audience. The publish date is your hard deadline. Everything before it has to be finished. You need this date locked in weeks in advance so you can plan backwards from it.

Promotion Phase: After publishing, you share clips, send emails, post on social media, and notify any sponsors. This phase is flexible but benefits from being tracked so you don't forget to promote episodes.

Working Backwards from Publish Date

The key insight is that you need to work backwards from your publish date. If Episode 5 is scheduled to publish on Friday, that means show notes must be complete by Wednesday. That means editing must be complete by Tuesday. That means recording must happen by Monday. If your recording runs long, you immediately know editing gets compressed. If you recognize the squeeze, you can either reschedule the recording or push the publish date.

Without explicit tracking, you realize at 4pm Thursday that the episode still isn't edited and it's supposed to publish tomorrow. You panic. You rush the editing. Quality suffers. Or you miss the publish date and disappoint your audience.

With tracking, you see Thursday morning that editing is behind, and you have time to address it. Either you find extra editing time Thursday, or you have a conversation about moving the publish date before your audience expects a new episode.

Managing Multiple Episodes

This becomes critical when you're producing multiple episodes per week. If you publish two episodes weekly, you typically have two recordings scheduled per week, two concurrent editing projects, two show notes documents in progress, and two publish dates happening at different times. A single tracking system showing where each episode sits in the pipeline prevents mistakes.

You can see at a glance: Episode 7 is being recorded this week, Episode 8 is in editing and due to finish by Friday, Episode 9 publishes Monday morning, and Episode 10 needs recording scheduled. Each episode has clear deadlines. Each person on the team (if you have one) knows what they're responsible for and when.

Pro tip: Build in buffer time. If your typical edit takes 3 days, set the editing deadline for 4 days after recording. If show notes usually take 1 day, give 1.5 days. This buffer catches delays without cascading into missed publish dates.

Why It Matters

Consistency builds audience loyalty. If your listeners know new episodes arrive every Friday at 9am, that's a promise. Missing that deadline occasionally is forgettable. Missing it regularly damages trust. Explicit tracking of production timelines ensures you keep that promise. You know weeks in advance which episodes publish when, and you manage the workflow to hit those dates reliably.
