Managing Multiple Podcast Shows Without Burning Out

2026-08-11

← Back to the Complete Podcast Production Guide

Running one podcast is manageable. Running two is double the work, but many successful creators do it. Running three or more becomes impossible without systematic tracking. The problem isn't the content creation itself—it's keeping track of who's supposed to appear on which show, when each show records, which deadlines belong to which podcast, and which sponsorship deals are tied to which audience.

Podcasters often drift into running multiple shows organically. You launch your main show. It gets an audience. Listeners ask for a bonus spin-off. You launch that. Then you get invited to co-host someone else's show. Suddenly you're managing three separate publication schedules, three separate guest pipelines, and three separate production timelines. Without organization, the workload explodes and quality drops.

The Multi-Show Challenge

Each show has its own requirements. Show A publishes weekly on Mondays. Show B publishes bi-weekly on Thursdays. Show C is a guest co-hosting project with a chaotic monthly schedule. Each show has different guests, different sponsorships, different recording formats. If you're tracking everything in separate spreadsheets or worse, in scattered email, you spend half your time context-switching and remembering which details belong to which show.

More critically, guests get confused. You tell a guest they're recording on Tuesday at 2pm, but forget to specify which show. They show up, and you realize they're prepped for Show A when you were scheduling them for Show C. Email confirmations become novel-length as you clarify "Show A, not the other one." Recording sessions start late because of miscommunication. Listener trust erodes because episodes aren't consistent across the portfolio.

Sponsors also get frustrated. A sponsor gives you $1000 to run ads on "your main show," but your main show is ambiguous if you're running three. Are they paying for Show A, Show B, or both? When do their ads run? If they run ads across the wrong show, they reach the wrong audience and get no conversions. They blame you. They don't sponsor again.

The Unified Tracking Solution

The solution is a single tracker for all shows, with a column that identifies which show each episode belongs to. Now you can sort by show and see the pipeline for each one independently, or see all shows at once to manage capacity.

Example view—all shows together:

Episode 45 (Show A): Records Tuesday, Publishes Monday next week

Episode 10 (Show B): Records Wednesday, Publishes Saturday

Episode 2 (Show C): Records two weeks out, Publishes unknown

From this view, you can see Tuesday's recording schedule is locked. Wednesday's open but will get filled. You have capacity to do two recordings this week without doubling up on a single day.

Example view—filtered to Show A only:

You see Show A's next five episodes, which guests are confirmed, which deadlines are coming up, and which episodes need sponsorship details added. The co-host of Show A can also view this filtered list without seeing the noise of Shows B and C.

Specific Workflows for Multi-Show Management

Weekly standup: Monday morning, filter the tracker by show. Spend five minutes reviewing Show A's pipeline, five minutes on Show B, five minutes on Show C. You know exactly what's happening across your portfolio and spot conflicts immediately.

Guest confirmation: When a guest confirms, you specify which show they're appearing on. Their email now has "Confirmed for Show A, Episode 47, Tuesday at 2pm" instead of ambiguous language. No confusion. They show up prepped for the right show.

Recording scheduling: You can see which days have existing recordings scheduled and which are free. You never double-book yourself because the tracker shows all shows in one view. You also see "I've got three Show A recordings this month and only one Show C. Let me balance the pipeline."

Sponsorship management: When a sponsor gives you money, you specify which show(s) they're sponsoring. Their ads appear on those episodes. You can see "Show A has Acme as sponsor for Episodes 40-42" and "Show B has Acme as sponsor for Episodes 15-17" simultaneously. Different audiences, separate commitments, clear tracking.

Publication scheduling: You know Show A publishes Mondays, Show B publishes Thursdays, Show C is monthly. You set publish dates accordingly and work backwards from each deadline. Your release calendar is visible and conflicts are obvious before they happen.

Capacity and Burnout Prevention

Multi-show tracking prevents the most dangerous problem: taking on too much. Without a unified view, you mentally feel like you're doing "a couple shows" when you're actually recording 8-10 episodes a month. You feel exhausted but don't understand why. With tracking, you see exactly how many recording sessions you're committing to. If it's unsustainable, you spot it and adjust before burning out.

You can say to a new guest or sponsor: "I'm running three shows on this schedule. Here's capacity available." You can show them the tracker and prove you're serious and organized. That professionalism leads to better guest matches and higher sponsorship rates.

Scaling Sustainably

The only way to scale to multiple shows sustainably is to stop tracking shows separately and start tracking them together. One unified system for all shows prevents context-switching, eliminates confusion, and lets you manage capacity intelligently.
